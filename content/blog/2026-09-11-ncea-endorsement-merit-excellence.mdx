**Most students who miss an endorsement miss it by a handful of credits, in a subject they never thought to push.** They had 46 credits at Merit or above, needed 50, and spent October studying the subject they were already best at.

Endorsement is the thing that separates "passed NCEA" from "passed NCEA well." It's what scholarship panels, competitive university programmes, and employers actually look at. And the maths behind it is simple enough that you can work out today, with eight weeks to go, exactly which exams to push.

## Two kinds of endorsement

There are two separate awards. You can get either, both, or neither.

**Certificate endorsement** is for the whole level. Your NCEA Level 1, 2 or 3 certificate gets "endorsed with Merit" or "endorsed with Excellence."

**Course endorsement** is for one subject. Your Level 2 Chemistry, say, gets endorsed with Merit or Excellence, independently of everything else.

They're calculated differently, and the strategy for each is different.

## Certificate endorsement: the 50-credit rule

To get your certificate endorsed with **Merit**, you need 50 credits at Merit or Excellence, at the certificate's level or above.

To get it endorsed with **Excellence**, you need 50 credits at Excellence, at the certificate's level or above.

A few things follow from this:

- Excellence credits count towards Merit endorsement. If you have 30 credits at Excellence and 20 at Merit, that's 50 at Merit-or-better, so you get Merit endorsement.
- Achieved credits count towards passing the level but contribute nothing to endorsement.
- Higher-level credits count down, same as for passing. Level 3 Excellence credits count towards a Level 2 Excellence endorsement.
- It's 50 out of the 60 you need to pass, but you'll usually earn more than 60 in a year. Every extra standard you achieve at Merit or Excellence is another chance at the 50.

<Callout type="info">
Certificate endorsement is worked out on all your credits at that level, across all subjects, internals and externals together. A Merit in Geography and an Excellence in Maths both go into the same pot.
</Callout>

## Course endorsement: 14 credits in one subject

To get a subject endorsed, you need 14 credits at Merit or Excellence in that subject, in a single school year, including at least 3 credits from external assessment and at least 3 from internal assessment.

For Excellence course endorsement, all 14 need to be at Excellence. For Merit, they need to be at Merit or better.

The "3 external and 3 internal" rule is the one that catches people. You can have 14 Excellence credits from internals alone and not get the endorsement, because you haven't got 3 from an external. A handful of subjects with no externals, like Physical Education, Religious Studies and Level 3 Visual Arts, have their own arrangements. For everything else, the November exam is required.

That is the most important sentence in this article for a Year 12 or 13 student in September: **course endorsement in almost every subject needs at least one external at Merit or Excellence.** Your internals have already decided whether you're in range. The external decides whether you get it.

## Do the maths on your own credits

Before you decide what to study, sit down with your credit summary and do this.

### For certificate endorsement

1. Add up your credits already reported at Merit or Excellence this year (and any higher-level ones from last year that count down).
2. Add up the ones at Excellence only.
3. Subtract each from 50.
4. List every external you're sitting with its credit value.

Now you have two gaps, Merit and Excellence, and a list of what's still available. If your Merit gap is 8 and you're sitting a 4-credit and a 5-credit external you're already close to Merit in, that's a realistic target. If your Excellence gap is 30 and you have 15 external credits left, Excellence endorsement isn't happening this year. Stop chasing it and put the effort into Merit.

### For course endorsement

For each subject:

1. Count this year's credits at Merit or better in that subject. Then count Excellence only.
2. Note whether at least 3 of them are from an internal. (Usually yes by September.)
3. Note what external credits are still to come.

If a subject is at 11 Merit-or-better credits from internals and the external is worth 4, one Merit in November gets you the course endorsement. That's a very different priority from a subject sitting at 6.

## Where endorsement credits actually come from

Think about the sources in order of how much you can still influence them.

**Internals already reported.** Done. Nothing to do here.

**Internals still to be assessed or resubmitted.** If your school allows a resubmission or you have a late internal, this is the cheapest endorsement credit available. Ask.

**Externals.** This is where the rest comes from. And unlike internals, you know the format, you know the standard, and you have eight weeks. If you understand [what examiners actually look for at Merit and Excellence](/blog/achieved-merit-excellence-what-examiners-look-for), moving one grade band in an external is a realistic target for most students in most subjects.

## Strategy: which externals to push

You can't push everything. Pick using these rules.

**Push where you're closest, not where you're weakest.** A subject where you're a solid Merit and occasionally hit Excellence in practice is the one to push to Excellence. A subject where you're scraping Achieved is not going to reach Excellence in eight weeks, and trying will cost you the realistic gains elsewhere.

**Weight by credits.** Between two subjects you're equally close in, push the external worth more credits. A 6-credit Merit closes a bigger gap than a 3-credit one.

**Check what the endorsement is actually for.** If you need Excellence course endorsement in Calculus for a scholarship application, that one external outranks a marginal certificate endorsement. If nothing specific depends on it, go for the certificate endorsement, which is what most people see.

**Don't push more than three.** Three externals, each targeted one grade up, is a plan. Six is a wish list.

**Everything else gets maintenance.** One session a week to make sure you don't slip. That's it.

Once you've picked, the [8-week NCEA study plan](/blog/how-to-study-for-ncea-exams) gives you the structure: find the specific topics costing you marks in each push subject, drill them with [retrieval practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testing_effect), then sit timed papers until the higher grade is what you produce by default, not on a good day.

## What moves a grade in an external

Since the whole strategy comes down to moving one or more externals up a band, it's worth being blunt about what that takes.

Getting from Achieved to Merit is usually about **depth**: explaining why, not just what. Linking a cause to an effect. Showing working, not just an answer.

Getting from Merit to Excellence is usually about **judgement**: justifying a choice, evaluating alternatives, seeing the connection the question was really pointing at, and saying so explicitly.

Neither is about knowing more content. Most students who get Merit knew enough for Excellence. They didn't write it down in the form the standard asks for. That is fixable, and it's fixable with practice questions that are marked honestly against the standard, so you can see exactly which band each answer landed in and why.

## Common questions

**Can I get certificate endorsement without any course endorsements?** Yes. They're independent. Fifty Merit credits spread across six subjects with none reaching 14 in a single subject still gives you Merit certificate endorsement.

**Can I get a course endorsement without certificate endorsement?** Yes. Fourteen Excellence credits in one subject and Achieved everywhere else gives you an Excellence course endorsement and an unendorsed certificate.

**Does endorsement carry over years?** Certificate endorsement is per level, calculated once. Course endorsement is per subject, per year.

**Where's the official rule?** [NZQA's NCEA site](https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/). If this article and NZQA ever disagree, NZQA wins.

## Do the count today

Count your Merit and Excellence credits. Work out both gaps. Pick up to three externals to push. Then study those, properly, starting this week. Most missed endorsements aren't about ability. They're about students who never did the count.
