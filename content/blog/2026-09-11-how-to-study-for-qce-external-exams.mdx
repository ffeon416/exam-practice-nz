**Most Queensland Year 12s treat the external exam like one more assessment. In Maths and Science, it's half your subject.**

By the time externals arrive in late October, you've already done three internal assessments. You're tired. It feels like the year is mostly done. And then one exam, sat in a hall with hundreds of other students, decides 50% of your result in Maths and Science subjects and 25% in most others.

That's not one more assessment. That's the biggest single thing you'll do all year. Here's how to prepare for it properly, starting six weeks out.

## How QCE externals actually work

Quick refresher so we're on the same page. The [Queensland Certificate of Education](https://www.qcaa.qld.edu.au/) is run by QCAA. General subjects have three internal assessments during Year 12 and one external assessment at the end, set and marked by QCAA rather than your school.

The external's weight depends on the subject:

| Subject type | External weight | What that means |
|---|---|---|
| Most Maths and Science subjects | 50% | Half your subject result rides on one exam |
| Most other General subjects | 25% | Still a quarter, and it's the only part your school doesn't mark |

Your ATAR is calculated by [QTAC](https://www.qtac.edu.au/) from your best five subject results. So a strong external in a 50% subject can move your ATAR more than any internal assessment you sat all year.

QCAA publishes the exam timetable each year. We won't put dates here because they change, so check the QCAA site and write yours on the wall.

## Why the external matters more than it feels like it does

Three reasons students under-prepare for it.

**It's at the end, when you're done.** Internal assessments come with deadlines and teacher reminders. The external is weeks after your last class. Nobody is chasing you. It's the assessment that's easiest to sleepwalk into.

**It's marked by strangers.** Your school knows you. QCAA's markers don't. There's no benefit of the doubt, no "they clearly understood it, they just phrased it oddly". You get the marks the marking guide says you get.

**Your internals don't tell you how you'll do.** Internal assessments are often open-ended tasks, extended responses, or investigations done with time and resources. The external is closed-book, timed, and covers the whole course. Being good at one says surprisingly little about the other.

That last point is the important one. Plenty of students with strong internal results go into the external with no evidence at all of how they'll perform in exam conditions. If you haven't read [why practice beats re-reading](/blog/testing-effect-practice-beats-rereading), it's the reason this plan is built around sitting papers rather than reviewing notes.

## The six-week plan

Six weeks before your first external. If you're reading this closer than that, compress it, but keep the order.

### Weeks 6 and 5: find out where you actually are

Don't start by revising. Start by measuring.

- **Download the past external papers and marking guides** for each of your subjects from QCAA. They're free and they're the real thing. This is the single most valuable resource you have.
- **Sit one full past paper per subject, timed, closed-book.** Do it badly if you have to. The point is the number, not the feeling.
- **Mark it strictly against QCAA's marking guide.** Not against what you meant. Against what you wrote. If the guide wants three points and you gave two, that's two.
- **Build a gap list per subject.** Not "I'm bad at Chemistry". Specific: "lose marks on equilibrium calculations", "can't structure the extended response in Modern History".

By the end of week 5 you should know, for every subject, roughly what mark you'd get today and the five or six topics costing you the most.

### Weeks 4 and 3: attack the gap list

This is the bulk of the work, and it's all [retrieval practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testing_effect) on weak topics. Not re-reading.

- **One topic per session.** Attempt questions on it (from past papers, your textbook, or AI-generated exam-style questions), check against the marking guide, write down what you got wrong.
- **Use the marking guide as a study text.** For extended responses especially, QCAA's marking guides tell you exactly what the difference is between a mid-band and a top-band answer. Read them like a cheat sheet, because that's what they are.
- **Space it.** Revisit each weak topic three or four times across the fortnight rather than once for a whole afternoon. Our [spaced repetition guide](/blog/spaced-repetition-for-exams-student-guide) covers why, but the short version is that [the forgetting curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forgetting_curve) doesn't care how long your first session was.
- **Keep a running mistakes list.** By week 3 it becomes your most personalised revision document.

### Weeks 2 and 1: exam conditions, every time

Now you shift from topics to papers.

- **One full timed paper every two or three days**, alternating subjects. Same time limit, same conditions, same equipment you'll have in the hall.
- **Mark every one strictly.** Track the score. It should be climbing. If it isn't for a particular subject, go back to the gap list for that subject.
- **Practise the paper's rhythm.** Know how long you have per mark. Know which section you'll start with. Know what you'll do when you hit a question you can't do (mark it, move on, come back).
- **Taper in the final three days.** Light review of your summary notes and mistakes list. No new material. Sleep.

<Callout type="info">
If you're short on past papers for a subject (newer subjects have fewer years available), AI-generated exam-style questions on your weak topics fill the gap well, as long as they're marked honestly. Just verify anything surprising against your textbook or a real QCAA marking guide.
</Callout>

## Using QCAA's papers properly

A past paper is worth a lot more than one attempt. Here's how to get the most out of each one.

**First pass:** timed, closed-book, as above. This is the measurement.

**Second pass, a day or two later:** go through every question you lost marks on. Redo it with the textbook open. Write the correct method in your own words in your mistakes list.

**Third pass, a week or more later:** redo just the questions you got wrong, timed, closed-book. If you get them now, they're fixed. If you don't, they go back on the list.

**Read the marking guide for the questions you got right, too.** You'll often find you got the answer with sloppy working that would only earn partial marks from a stranger. Fix that now, not in the hall.

## Common traps in the external

These come up every year.

**Running out of time on the extended response.** In subjects with a long written section, students spend too long on the short-answer questions and leave 15 minutes for the part worth the most. Set time checkpoints and stick to them.

**Answering the question you wish they'd asked.** Underline the command word. "Evaluate" is not "describe". "Determine" is not "explain". The marking guide is built around the command word, and an excellent answer to the wrong command word scores poorly.

**Not showing working in Maths and Science.** With 50% of your result on the line, working marks matter enormously. A right answer with no method can score less than a wrong answer with a correct method. Write every step.

**Hedging.** "It could be A or B depending on..." is a zero when the question wants a decision. Commit. Justify. Move on.

**Ignoring the stimulus.** Many QCE questions come with a data table, graph or source. If the question refers to it, your answer needs to refer to it too, explicitly. Markers look for it.

## Why honest marking is the whole game

Everything in this plan depends on one thing: when you mark your practice, you mark it the way QCAA will.

That's harder than it sounds. It's human to give yourself the mark for an answer you "basically" got. It feels fine in the moment. It's disastrous in October, because you walk in believing you're at a level you're not.

So use the official marking guides ruthlessly. If you're using anything else to mark your practice, a tutor, a study group, an AI tool, make sure it's equally ruthless. Wrong is wrong. Hedges score zero. Working and answer are marked separately. If a marker is being kind to you, it's not helping you.

## The short version

- The external is 50% of Maths and Science subjects, 25% of most others, and marked by strangers.
- Six weeks out: sit a QCAA past paper per subject, timed, and build a gap list.
- Weeks 4 to 3: retrieval practice on the gap list, using QCAA marking guides as study texts.
- Weeks 2 to 1: full timed papers every few days, marked strictly, then taper.
- Show working, obey the command word, use the stimulus, never hedge.
- Mark yourself the way QCAA will, or the plan doesn't work.
