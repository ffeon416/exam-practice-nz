**Every year, students pass NCEA Level 3 and still don't get into university.** They had the credits. They had the grades. They were missing one line on a checklist nobody showed them until January.

University Entrance (UE) is a separate qualification from NCEA Level 3. You can have one without the other. Most students are on track for both, but "most" isn't "all", and the students who miss out usually miss by a single subject or a handful of literacy credits they could have picked up in Year 12.

Here is the whole thing in plain English, the traps, and how to check where you stand right now.

## The UE checklist

To get University Entrance you need all four of these:

| Requirement | What it means |
|---|---|
| **NCEA Level 3** | 60 credits at Level 3 or above. |
| **Three approved subjects** | 14 credits at Level 3 in each of three subjects from NZQA's approved-subject list. |
| **UE literacy** | 10 credits at Level 2 or above: 5 in reading and 5 in writing. |
| **UE numeracy** | 10 credits at Level 1 or above in numeracy-related standards. |

That's the full list. Four lines. Every one of them has a trap in it.

The authoritative version, including the current approved-subject list and which standards count for reading, writing and numeracy, is on [NZQA's NCEA site](https://www2.nzqa.govt.nz/ncea/). Check it directly for anything that affects your own entries. Rules can change, and your school's course guide may be out of date.

## The four traps

### Trap 1: "approved subjects" is a specific list

Not every subject you can take at Level 3 counts towards UE. NZQA publishes an approved list. Common academic subjects like English, Mathematics (including Calculus and Statistics), Biology, Chemistry, Physics, History, Geography, Economics and Accounting are on it. Some vocational, unit-standard-based, or school-designed courses are not.

The problem: a student picks three subjects they enjoy in Year 13, two are approved and one isn't, and they don't find out until results come out. Check every one of your Level 3 subjects against the list at the start of the year, not the end. If in doubt, ask your dean, and ask them to show you the list rather than tell you from memory.

### Trap 2: 14 credits in *each* of three subjects, not 42 credits spread around

This is the one that catches the most people. UE doesn't ask for 42 credits across three subjects. It asks for at least 14 in *each* of three. Twenty credits in English, twenty in History and nine in Biology does not qualify, even though it's 49 credits in approved subjects.

Fourteen credits usually means passing most of the standards in that subject, including at least one external. If you're sitting on ten credits in a subject going into November, that subject's externals are where your UE lives.

### Trap 3: UE literacy has to be at Level 2 or above

Level 1 literacy credits (including the co-requisite you did for NCEA Level 1) do not count for UE literacy. You need 5 reading credits and 5 writing credits from standards at Level 2 or higher, and they have to be standards NZQA has flagged as counting for UE reading or writing.

Most students collect these through Level 2 English without noticing. But students who dropped English after Year 11, or who did a Level 2 English course built on standards that don't carry the UE literacy flag, can arrive in Year 13 short. It is fixable in Year 13 with the right standards, but only if you know before November.

### Trap 4: numeracy is easy to forget and easy to get

UE numeracy is 10 credits at Level 1 or above in numeracy standards. Almost everyone who did Level 1 maths has this already. The trap is a student who did an alternative pathway at Level 1 and assumes they're fine. Check it once. It takes two minutes on your NZQA record.

<Callout type="warning">
"I passed Level 3" and "I have UE" are different sentences. If someone tells you you're fine because your credits are on track, ask them which of the four requirements they've actually checked.
</Callout>

## How to check where you stand mid-year

You don't have to guess. Do this in one sitting, ideally today:

1. **Log in to your NZQA learner account** and pull up your results. Everything you've been credited so far is there.
2. **List your three intended UE subjects.** Confirm each is on the approved list. If one isn't, decide now whether you can swap it or add a fourth subject.
3. **For each of the three, add up Level 3 credits earned so far** (internals already reported) **and credits still available** (remaining internals plus externals). If earned plus available is under 14, that subject can't get you there. If earned is under 14 but earned plus available is over, the externals are now must-pass.
4. **Check literacy.** Find your Level 2 (or higher) reading and writing credits. Five of each. If you're short, ask your English teacher which remaining standards this year carry the UE literacy flag.
5. **Check numeracy.** Ten credits at Level 1 or above. Done in thirty seconds.
6. **Check Level 3 overall.** Sixty credits at Level 3 or above. Remember that Level 3 credits from any subject count towards this total, including ones outside your three UE subjects.

Write the result down as a table with a "have", "need" and "where it's coming from" column. Bring it to your dean. Deans deal with UE every year and will spot problems you can't, but only if you ask before results day.

If you're not sure how credits and levels fit together, [how many credits you need to pass NCEA](/blog/ncea-credits-explained-how-many-to-pass) covers the foundations.

## What to prioritise in November if you're short

Externals are the last credits available in the year, and for a lot of Year 13 students they decide UE. If you've done the check above and you're short somewhere, here's how to think about the exam period:

**Short in one subject's 14 credits?** That subject's externals are the priority. Every hour of study should go there first. It doesn't matter if your other subjects are strong. UE needs all three.

**Short on Level 3 total but fine on subjects?** Any Level 3 external will do. Put the time into the papers where you're closest to Achieved. A grade you can realistically move from Not Achieved to Achieved is worth more to you than pushing an Achieved to Merit.

**Short on literacy?** This is usually not solvable in an exam unless you're sitting a Level 2 or 3 English external that carries the reading or writing flag. Talk to your teacher now. There may be an internal still open, or a standard you can be entered for.

**On track for everything?** Then November is about grades, not qualification. If you're chasing endorsement, see [NCEA endorsement explained](/blog/ncea-endorsement-merit-excellence). Just keep an eye on the 14-credit subject minimums. Don't skip an external in a subject that's sitting on exactly 14 credits, because if a reported internal is later changed, you'll have no buffer.

Whatever the situation, the study method is the same. The credits come from questions answered correctly under exam conditions, so practise under exam conditions. [How to study for NCEA exams](/blog/how-to-study-for-ncea-exams) lays out an 8-week plan.

## A quick word on university-specific requirements

UE gets you eligible to apply. It does not guarantee a place in every programme. Some degrees, particularly health sciences, engineering and some law and commerce pathways, have their own entry requirements: specific subjects, rank scores based on your best credits, or guaranteed-entry thresholds. Those are set by each university, not NZQA. Check the programme page for the course you want, and check it in Year 12 rather than Year 13, because subject choices you make now can close doors later.

## Do the check today

Six steps, one sitting, one table. Most students will find they're fine and can stop worrying. A few will find a gap while there's still time to close it. Either way, you'll know, and knowing in September beats finding out in January.
